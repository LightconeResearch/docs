A figure rarely tells you which data cuts, assumptions and software versions produced it. Finding
that out can mean reading scripts, comparing notebooks and asking the author what they remember.
Working with an AI agent makes it easy to produce more results, but someone still needs to check
how they were obtained.

The Lightcone Research Stack helps you keep that record while you work. You describe the research
question and review the methodological choices with your agent. The analysis specification records
those choices; the execution tools record how the outputs were made; the report refers to those
outputs when you write up the results.

You can use the CLI without an agent, write ASTRA without Lightcone, or publish a MySTRA report
without JupyterLab. The [quick start](/) follows the agent-assisted workflow.

## What the record lets you check

Suppose changing a covariance estimate doubles the uncertainty on a measurement. In ASTRA, that
choice can be a named decision with two options. Each configuration is a universe. The CLI runs
both and records which inputs and choices produced each output. A reader can then see which
uncertainty the report uses and inspect the alternative.

The [first-analysis tutorial](/guides/first-analysis) works through this example with supernova
data. The longer-term aim is for results to be usable in subsequent research with their evidence
and assumptions still attached; [ASTRA](/astra#how-claims-chain-together) explains that connection.

These records help you review the work. They do not establish that a model is appropriate, that
the code implements it correctly, or that a conclusion follows from the results. Those remain
scientific judgments for you and your collaborators.
