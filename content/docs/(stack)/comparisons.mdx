Research is shared today as a paper, sometimes with a code repository, and neither records every choice
behind a result. Lightcone Research starts from the idea that agent-driven research should also produce a
record of the analysis: its inputs, outputs, decisions and evidence, complete enough for someone else to
reproduce and extend it.

The Lightcone Stack keeps that record in `astra.yaml`, written in [ASTRA](/astra), an
[open specification](https://astra-spec.org). You can review, reproduce and cite the analysis as a piece
of work in its own right. This page compares the stack with other tools for AI-assisted research.

## At a glance

| Tool                        | Main focus                                                         |
| --------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| Lightcone Stack             | Scientific decisions, their evidence, and an open analysis record  |
| Claude Science, OpenScience | Research workbenches that track the provenance of artifacts        |
| Kosmos, The AI Scientist    | Autonomous research, from question to report                       |
| Snakemake, Nextflow         | Defining and running reproducible pipelines                        |

## Provenance and decisions

Research workbenches such as [Claude Science](https://www.anthropic.com/news/claude-science-ai-workbench)
and [OpenScience](https://github.com/synthetic-sciences/openscience) save versioned figures and other
files, with the conversation, code and logs that produced them. That tells you how each file was made.
ASTRA also records why: the scientific decisions behind the analysis, the options considered for each,
and the literature that supports them.

## Workflow managers

[Snakemake](https://snakemake.readthedocs.io/) and [Nextflow](https://www.nextflow.io/) are the
established way to define and run reproducible pipelines. They capture how each output is computed, but
not the scientific reasoning behind the pipeline, and they weren't designed with agents in mind. The
Lightcone Stack is built for agents and records the decisions and evidence behind each step. You can use
the two together, as the next section shows.

## Bring your own workspace

Claude Science and OpenScience come with their own agents and browser-based workspaces. With the
Lightcone Stack, you keep the agent and the tools you already use.

The stack covers an analysis from start to finish, but its tools are independent, and none requires the
others. You can record an analysis in `astra.yaml` and run it with your own scripts or workflow manager
instead of the Lightcone CLI. You could also ask the agent in Claude Science to work from `astra.yaml`.

## Researchers stay in the loop

The Lightcone Stack is for researchers who want to understand every step of their analysis and stay
responsible for it. Autonomous AI scientists such as
[Kosmos](https://advances.edisonscientific.com/research/announcing-kosmos/) and Sakana's
[AI Scientist](https://sakana.ai/ai-scientist/) pursue a research question end to end on their own. You
receive their conclusions without having made the choices behind them.

## Owned by the research community

The Lightcone Stack is open source. We believe the academic community should own, maintain and extend
the tools it relies on. Claude Science and Kosmos, by contrast, are proprietary and require a
subscription.
