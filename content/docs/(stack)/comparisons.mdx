Here we compare the purpose of the Lightcone Stack with other tools for AI-assisted science.

The central idea behind the Lightcone Stack is that agent-driven research should produce a record of the analysis, covering
its inputs, outputs, choices, and evidence. Today, research is shared mainly as a paper and sometimes a code
repository, and neither captures every important aspect of the research. A full record enables research to be
reproduced and extended.

The Lightcone Stack records these in an `astra.yaml` file, an [open specification](https://astra-spec.org) that
allows an analysis to be reviewed, reproduced, and cited as a piece of work in its own right.

[Claude Science](https://www.anthropic.com/news/claude-science-ai-workbench) and
[OpenScience](https://github.com/synthetic-sciences/openscience) are AI workbenches that save versioned figures and
other files, along with the conversation, code, and logs that produced them. ASTRA goes beyond surface-level
provenance tracking. Its specification focuses not just on inputs and outputs, but also on the scientific decisions behind an analysis,
why they were made, and the literature that supports them.

Workflow managers such as [Snakemake](https://snakemake.readthedocs.io/) help researchers define and run reproducible
pipelines but were not designed with agent-driven science in mind, whereas the Lightcone Stack is built for agents and also
records the scientific decisions and evidence behind each step.

## Bring your own workspace

Tools like Claude Science and OpenScience bring their own agents and browser-based workspaces, whereas
Lightcone lets users keep the existing tools and agents that fit their workflow.

The Lightcone Stack covers an analysis from start to finish, but its components are independent, and none of them
requires the others. For example, a researcher can record an analysis in `astra.yaml` and still run it with their
own scripts or workflow manager instead of the Lightcone CLI. They could even ask Claude Science to use `astra.yaml`!

## Researchers stay in the loop

The Lightcone Stack is built for researchers who want to understand and remain responsible for every step of their
analysis. Compare this with a growing number of independent AI scientists, such as
[Kosmos](https://advances.edisonscientific.com/research/announcing-kosmos/) and Sakana's [AI
Scientist](https://sakana.ai/ai-scientist/), which are designed to pursue their own research questions end to end. With these tools, researchers are left with conclusions they did not shape and may not fully understand.

## Owned by the research community

Finally, the Lightcone Stack is open source. We believe that the tools the academic community relies on should be
owned, maintained, and extended by that community. Claude Science and Kosmos, by contrast, are proprietary and
require subscriptions.

## At a glance

| Tool                     | What it does                                                                                                   |
| ------------------------ | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Lightcone Stack          | Records analyses in ASTRA spec, with tools to write, run, and share them with your own agent. Open source. |
| Claude Science           | Browser-based research workbench in which Claude runs analyses and records provenance for each file. Proprietary.            |
| OpenScience              | Browser-based research workbench with its own research agents. Open source.                       |
| Kosmos                   | AI scientist that carries out research end to end and returns a report. Proprietary.                          |
| The AI Scientist                   | AI scientist that carries out research end to end and returns a report. Open source.                       |
